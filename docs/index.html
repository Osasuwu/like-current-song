Like Current Song

Like the song you are hearing without unlocking your phone.

Home Privacy Terms Source

A song you love comes on while the phone is in your pocket. Pause and resume it with your headphone button and it is saved to your Spotify Liked Songs or your YouTube Music likes. You don't unlock the phone, look at the screen or open the music app, and a short tone confirms the like.

At the computer, a global keyboard shortcut does the same: Ctrl+Shift+Alt+W likes whatever is playing, even with Spotify minimized or in the background.

What it does

  • Android — works with the screen off and the phone locked. It watches the pause/play state of whatever is playing, so wired or Bluetooth headphones, earbud taps, a smartwatch or a car stereo can all trigger it. The pattern is configurable.
  • Windows — a tray app with a global hotkey to like the current track, and a second one to discard it.
  • macOS and Linux — a like-current-song like-once command you bind to any shortcut.
  • Spotify and YouTube Music — on Android it can pick whichever one is actually playing. A like can also go to a playlist of yours instead of, or as well as, the service's own likes.
  • Optional rules — take the track out of a Discover Weekly archive playlist, promote it to a "best" playlist after N likes, follow an artist after N liked tracks. The counts live in a Google Sheet you own, so phone and computer see the same numbers.

Get it

Download the Android APK

There is no Play Store listing, so Android will warn you about an app from outside the store — that is expected for a sideloaded APK. For the desktop tool, clone the repository and run the installer (install.ps1 on Windows, install.sh on macOS and Linux).

  • Setup guide — connecting Spotify or YouTube Music
  • All releases and changelog
  • Known limitations — some phones stop background apps once swiped away

Bring your own credentials

The project does not ship shared API keys. Spotify and Google both require each person to register their own application, so the setup guide walks you through creating one and pasting its client ID into the app. Those credentials stay on your device, and there is no server run by this project — see the privacy policy.
